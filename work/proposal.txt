Strategy Proposal: Insider Signal Earnings Momentum Trader
(ISEM)
Prepared for: Trading Algorithm Development Team
Status: Draft for Review
Document Version: 1.0
1. Executive Summary
This proposal outlines a new short-term swing trading algorithm — the Insider Signal
Earnings Momentum Trader (ISEM) — built on the premise that corporate insiders and
members of Congress, when purchasing stock in the period between earnings reports,
often possess informational advantages about the company's next reported results. By
cross-referencing mandatory public disclosures of such purchases against the upcoming
earnings calendar, the algorithm identiﬁes stocks likely to rise on their next earnings
report. ISEM takes a single long position ahead of each qualifying earnings event, holds the
position through the market's processing of that report, and exits at close the following day.
All trades are bracket orders with a stop-loss. If no high-conviction candidate exists on a
given day, the algorithm holds cash.
2. Investment Thesis
When a company executive purchases a meaningful amount of their own company's stock
in the weeks leading up to an earnings report — particularly as that report approaches — it
is reasonable to infer that the executive has conﬁdence in results that have not yet been
made public. Public companies trading on major U.S. exchanges (NYSE, NASDAQ, etc.) are
required by the SEC to disclose such purchases via Form 4, typically within two business
days of the transaction. Members of Congress are separately required to disclose their stock
trades within 45 days of a transaction under the STOCK Act.
If a company beats earnings expectations, its stock price typically rises on or immediately
after the announcement. By systematically identifying stocks where insiders and Congress
members have been purchasing shares during the inter-earnings window — especially
with increasing frequency as the next earnings date approaches — the algorithm positions
itself to capture that post-earnings upside, while a strict stop-loss limits downside
exposure.

The algorithm will monitor purchases made by the following categories of individuals for

U.S. Senate members
Transaction Type Filter: The algorithm should only track open market purchases (Form 4
transaction code "P"). It should exclude option exercises, awards, gifts, and planned
purchases made under pre-scheduled 10b5-1 trading plans, as these do not reﬂect real-time
conviction. A ﬂag for 10b5-1 plan purchases is included in Form 4 ﬁlings and should be used
to ﬁlter these out.

5. Signal Timing Framework
The period between consecutive earnings reports is divided into distinct timing zones.
Insider purchases carry diﬀerent signal weight depending on when they occurred within
the inter-earnings cycle.
5.1 Post-Earnings Exclusion Window (Days 1–14 after prior earnings)
Duration: Approximately 14 calendar days (10 trading days) following the prior
earnings report date
Rationale: Purchases immediately after an earnings report are most likely a reaction
to newly revealed results — good results that are now public knowledge — rather than
forward-looking signals about the next earnings period. These purchases do not help
predict future, unreleased performance.
Treatment: Excluded entirely from the scoring algorithm.
Note: This window is a recommended starting point. The exact cutoﬀ (10, 14, or 21
days) should be validated and tuned during backtesting, and the architecture should
make this parameter easily adjustable.


--- PAGE 4 ---
5.2 Core Signal Window (Day 15 after prior earnings through Day 28 before next
earnings)
This is the primary zone of interest. Purchases here are most likely driven by genuine
conﬁdence in current business performance during the inter-earnings period.
Most publicly traded companies enforce formal trading blackout periods beginning
approximately 30 days before their earnings announcement. Purchases within the
Core Signal Window occur during the legally permissible open trading window and
carry strong credibility.
Treatment: Full scoring weight applied. See Section 6.
5.3 Late Signal Window (Day 28 through Day 3 before next earnings)
As the next earnings date approaches, insiders are increasingly likely to be
constrained by their company's blackout policy. Any purchases in this window
despite a typical blackout would be unusual and potentially indicate extremely high
conviction (though they also raise compliance questions).
In practice, some companies have shorter or no formal blackout periods, so late-
window purchases may simply reﬂect normal trading.
Treatment: Purchases in this window receive a signal boost — they are weighted
more heavily than Core Signal Window purchases, as proximity to the earnings date
with continued buying suggests heightened conﬁdence. The exact multiplier should
be calibrated during backtesting.
5.4 Final 3 Days Before Earnings (Day 3 to Day 0)
This is the algorithm's own entry zone. No new insider signal analysis is applied here;
the algorithm is executing, not still evaluating.

6.1 Scoring Dimensions
A. Insider Count (max 25 points)
Each unique insider or Congress member who made a qualifying purchase: +5 points
each, up to a maximum of 5 distinct buyers
B. Role Weighting (max 20 points)
At least one C-suite executive among buyers: +10 points
At least one board member or beneﬁcial owner among buyers: +5 points
At least one Congress member among buyers: +5 points (equal weight to corporate
insider per role tier)
C. Timing Quality (max 20 points)
Core Signal Window purchases present: +10 points
Late Signal Window purchases present: +20 points (replaces Core Window points if
both windows have purchases — Late Window is the stronger signal)
D. Velocity Score (max 20 points)
Multiple purchase events (across time) by any combination of insiders: +10 points
Increasing purchase dollar amounts across the inter-earnings period (trend upward):
+10 points
Velocity is calculated by dividing the inter-earnings window into thirds and
comparing purchase volume in each third
E. Congressional Lag Adjustment (up to +5 points)
If a congressional purchase was disclosed very recently but the actual transaction
date (per the ﬁling) falls squarely in the Late Signal Window, add +5 points to reﬂect
the recency of the underlying information
Total Maximum Score: 90 points

7. Daily Algorithm Workﬂow
The algorithm runs the following process each trading day:
Step 1: Earnings Calendar Pull (Pre-Market)
Query the earnings calendar for companies reporting earnings:
Today (if reporting after market close today)
Tomorrow (if reporting before market open or during market hours tomorrow)
Filter to companies listed on NYSE or NASDAQ only
Record each company's ticker, earnings report date, and report timing (BMO /
Intraday / AMC)
Step 2: Insider Data Pull
For each company identiﬁed in Step 1:
Retrieve their most recent prior earnings date
Pull all Form 4 open-market purchases ﬁled since that prior earnings date
Apply the 10b5-1 ﬁlter (exclude pre-planned purchases)
Pull congressional disclosures for the same ticker; translate disclosure dates to
transaction dates
Apply the Post-Earnings Exclusion Window (discard purchases within 14 days of
prior earnings)
Step 3: Score Each Candidate
Apply the scoring framework from Section 6 to each remaining company
Discard any company scoring below the minimum threshold


--- PAGE 7 ---
Step 4: Rank and Select
Rank all qualifying companies by their composite score (highest to lowest)
If a tie exists, use dollar volume of insider purchases as a tiebreaker
Select the top-ranked company as the trade candidate for the day
Step 5: Check Existing Position
If the algorithm currently holds a position that has not yet reached its exit trigger, do
not enter a new trade — wait for the current position to close ﬁrst before evaluating
the next candidate
Step 6: Execute or Hold Cash
If a qualifying candidate exists and no current position is held: execute the bracket
order (see Section 8)
If no candidate meets the threshold: hold cash for the day. Do not force a trade.
8. Trade Execution Rules
8.1 Position Sizing
Each position uses 50% of total available capital
The remaining 50% is held in cash at all times
This applies even when conviction is very high — no exceptions
8.2 Entry Timing
Earnings Report Timing Entry Time
Before Market Open (BMO) — tomorrow Before market close today
During Market Hours (Intraday) Before market close today
After Market Close (AMC) — today Before market close today
All entries target execution as close to market close as practical (e.g., within the ﬁnal 15–30
minutes of the session) to capture as much of the intraday price discovery as possible while
ensuring the position is established before the report.
8.3 Exit Timing


--- PAGE 8 ---
Earnings Report Timing Exit Time
Before Market Open (BMO) Market close on the day of the report
During Market Hours (Intraday) Market close on the day of the report
After Market Close (AMC) Market close on the next trading day
The algorithm does not attempt to sell into the post-earnings spike in real-time. Selling at
close allows the market a full session to process the earnings reaction before the position is
liquidated.
8.4 Weekend Holding
Positions may occasionally be held over weekends. This occurs when a company
reports earnings after close on a Friday; the exit is at close the following Monday.
The stop-loss bracket remains active, but gap risk over the weekend should be
understood as an inherent characteristic of this strategy (see Section 10).
8.5 Bracket Order Structure
Every trade is entered as a bracket order consisting of:
1. Market/Limit Entry Order — executed near market close on the entry day (see
Section 8.2)
2. Stop-Loss Order — a sell stop set at 4% below the entry price
This is a hard protective ﬂoor. If the stock falls 4% from entry before the
scheduled exit, the position is closed immediately
The 4% level is chosen to allow for normal overnight volatility while protecting
against meaningful adverse moves. This ﬁgure should be reviewed and adjusted
based on backtesting results
3. Primary Exit Order — a market-on-close (MOC) sell order for the designated exit
date (see Section 8.3)
The entry and primary exit are linked; the stop-loss is a standing resting order that cancels
automatically if the primary exit executes ﬁrst. All three legs are submitted together.
Gap Risk Acknowledgment: In cases where a stock opens signiﬁcantly below the stop-loss
trigger (e.g., due to a pre-market earnings miss), the stop order may execute at a price
meaningfully worse than 4% below entry. This is a known and accepted risk of overnight
holding and cannot be fully eliminated with a stop order. It is documented here for
transparency.

9. Capital Allocation and Throughput
Capital per trade: 50% of total available capital
Concurrent positions: Maximum 1 at any time
Cash ﬂoor: Never less than 50% in cash
Trade frequency: Approximately 1 trade per 1–3 trading days, depending on signal
availability
Holding period: Typically 1–3 trading days; up to 4 days including a weekend

10.2 What This Strategy Does Not Do
No short selling — the algorithm is strictly long-only
No leverage — positions are cash-funded only
No forced trading — the algorithm holds cash when no qualifying candidate exists

12. Recommended Implementation Phases
Phase 1 — Data Infrastructure (Weeks 1–3)
Build data pipelines for:
Yahoo Finance earnings calendar (via yfinance)
SEC EDGAR Form 4 ﬁlings (via sec-edgar-downloader)
House and Senate Stock Watcher APIs
Normalize all data into a uniﬁed internal schema keyed by ticker and date
Build the transaction-date extraction logic for congressional ﬁlings (handling the
disclosure lag)
Phase 2 — Signal Engine Development (Weeks 4–6)
Implement the timing window classiﬁer
Implement the composite scoring model (Section 6)
Build the daily workﬂow logic (Section 7)
Implement the ranking and selection logic (Section 9)
Phase 3 — Backtesting (Weeks 7–11)
Backtest against at least 5 years of historical earnings events (2019–2024)
Evaluate returns, win rate, average gain on winners, average loss on losers, and Sharpe
ratio
Use backtesting results to calibrate all open items from Section 11
Ensure stop-loss logic and bracket order simulation are included in the backtest
Phase 4 — Paper Trading (Weeks 12–19)
Run the algorithm in paper trading mode for a minimum of 2 months
Validate live data pipeline reliability
Conﬁrm execution timing is achievable with the broker's order types
Document edge cases (e.g., earnings date changes, trading halts, delistings)
Phase 5 — Live Deployment (Week 20+)
Deploy with real capital following satisfactory paper trading results
Monitor signal quality, execution quality, and P&L weekly
